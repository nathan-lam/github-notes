# Definition
A way of declaring two elements of a set as equivalent.




An equivalence relation is defined by the following properties:

- Reflexivity
	- a~a
	- It is self similar for all elements in the set
- Symmetry
	- a~b is the same as b~a
	- Relation goes both ways
- [[Transitivity]]
	- a~b, b~c -> a~c
	- Relation can transfer


# Examples



- Reflexive and symmetric but not transitive
	- Homophone words
		- Reflexive
			- cot is a homophone to cot
		- Symmetric
			- cot and caught being homophones is the same as caught and cot being homophones
		- Not Transitive
			- red and read being being homophones and read and reed being homophones does NOT make red and reed homophones
- Reflexive but neither symmetric nor transitive

- Symmetric but neither reflexive nor transitive
	- Coprime integers
		- Not Reflexive
			- 5 is not coprime to 5
		- Symmetric
			- 3 and 5 being coprime is the same as 5 and 3 being coprime
		- Not Transitive
			- 2 and 5 being coprime and 5 and 8 being coprime does NOT mean 2 and 8 are coprime
- Transitive but neither reflexive nor symmetric
	- Inequality comparison on the real numbers
		- Not reflexive
			- a < a is False
		- Not symmetric
			- (a < b) is different than (b < a)
		- Transitive
			- (a < b) and (b < a) implying (a < c) is true
